# ADR-0037: Corpus intake — extract, find the holes, ask, fill, then propose

## Status
Accepted. Adds to the front surface in the
[architecture brief](../architecture-brief.md); leaves
[ADR-0017](0017-conversation-is-the-product.md)'s approval gate and
[ADR-0035](0035-hierarchical-approval-and-blocking.md)'s node-level
approval exactly as they are.

## Context
The factory assumes a project's specifications are born in conversation.
The first real customer project did not work that way. `drones` arrived
with 43 specification documents already written — about 68,000 words of
prose in `moonbeam-specs/specs/drones/`, each with Problem, Behaviour,
Design, Acceptance and Open questions — and a repository that is still a
scaffold. Any buyer already shipping agent-written code will be in the same
position: the specifications exist, somewhere, in some shape, and they are
not ours.

The first attempt was to paste documents into an ordinary conversation.
It produced 68 nodes from two documents and exposed three problems:

- **A conversation holds a few documents, not a corpus.** Each turn saw
  what was pasted into it. A detail settled in document 12 was a hole in
  document 30, and got asked — or worse, guessed.
- **Holes were buried in prose.** The architect raised good questions, but
  as sentences in a reply. Nothing recorded which were answered, which
  were outstanding, or whether an answer had made it into the proposal.
- **Approval arrived before the questions were done.** A settled turn
  proposes, so an amendment could reach a reviewer carrying a detail
  nobody had decided.

The product decision behind this ADR is that **after intake the factory is
authoritative.** The source documents are imported once; from then on the
specifications change through the factory, and the originals may be frozen,
regenerated by `df.specs.export`, or deleted.

## Decision

**Intake is its own flow, in the order a person would do it: extract, find
holes, ask, fill — and only then propose.** A source document becomes an
ordinary amendment at the end; it never writes the graph, and approval is
whatever approval is (today `df.specs.approve`; per-node under ADR-0035).

**The whole corpus is in every extraction.** It sits in the cacheable half
of the system prompt, byte-identical across an intake, so each document's
extraction can settle a detail from any other document rather than ask a
person, and the corpus is paid for once rather than per document. At 43
documents and roughly 100k tokens it fits comfortably; a corpus that does
not fit is a retrieval problem for when one exists.

**A hole is a row, not a sentence.** Each extraction returns a draft (a
specdiff document restricted to creates and the edges between them) and a
list of holes, each one direct question with a closed `kind` — ambiguity,
contradiction, untestable, undefined_term, source_open_question, missing —
a quote, and which draft nodes depend on it. Holes become
`intake_questions` with a status: open, answered, deferred, resolved.

**Filling is re-extraction.** An answer is recorded, then the source is
extracted again with every answered and deferred question and its own
previous draft in front of the model. An open question the model returns
by id stays open; one it stops returning is resolved; a new hole is a new
question. Answered and deferred questions may not be raised again —
validation refuses the draft.

**A draft cannot be proposed while a person owes it an answer, or while it
does not yet reflect one they gave.** Open questions block. An answered
question whose `incorporated_in_revision` is null blocks. A deferred
question does not block, and later extractions are told not to guess at it.

**Every node cites its source.** The factory prefixes each draft node's
rationale with the source ref rather than trusting the prompt to, because
once the factory is authoritative the rationale is the only place "where
did this rule come from" remains answerable.

**The corpus is stored as submitted,** as one artifact per intake, with a
content hash per document. The originals can change or vanish; what every
extraction was made from cannot.

**Cross-document edges are not made during intake.** A draft may only link
its own nodes: nodes from another document are not in the graph until that
document's amendment is approved, so they have no ids to point at.
Relationships across documents are cited in rationales and become edges in
a linking pass after approval.

### Surface

```
df.intake.start(project_id, name, sources[{source_ref, title, content}])
df.intake.extract(source_id)          draft + holes; re-run after answering
df.intake.status(intake_id)           per-source status, nodes, question counts
df.intake.questions(intake_id, status?, source_id?)
df.intake.answer(question_id, answer)
df.intake.defer(question_id, reason)
df.intake.propose(source_id)          → amendment (Proposed)
```

Sources are submitted inline rather than read from a registered server:
the factory has no runtime MCP client yet (`ISpokeClient` is unimplemented),
and inline makes any corpus importable — a markdown repository, a wiki
export — with a thin driver (`scripts/intake.mjs`). A `specs`-domain source
server is the natural next shape once the client exists.

## Consequences
- Extraction calls are recorded under `stage_id = 'intake'`, with an
  `IntakeContextPack` per attempt that references the corpus artifact
  rather than copying it.
- Duplicate nodes across documents are possible — two documents stating
  one rule — and are left for the linking pass, which has the approved
  graph to compare against. Extraction is told to leave restatements to the
  document that owns them, which reduces but does not eliminate this.
- Drafts are mutable and are not append-only. That is deliberate: the
  append-only guarantees of ADR-0016 begin at the amendment, exactly where
  they begin for a conversation.
- The questions have no screen yet. They are answerable through the MCP
  surface today; the conversation screen is where they belong once it is
  wired.
